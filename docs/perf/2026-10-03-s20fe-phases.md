## Observations

- Phone solver shares are close to the desktop ones: dense 27 % (desktop 29.5), moving 44.5 % (46). s3-basket is lower at 30 to 36 % (desktop 38), and stress-still is higher at 33 % (21; on the phone it runs only 60 s, including the settling phase). Behaviour is 55 to 73 % of the phone tick.
- If the native port removed the whole solver, the tick would be about 10.2 ms on dense, 8.4 ms on basket, 10.1 ms on moving and 4.7 ms on still. Even a perfect port therefore leaves roughly 8 to 10 ms of GDScript behaviour per tick in the crowded fixtures. The port helps most in moving crowds (moving: 18.3 to about 11 ms with a 5 to 10x faster solver), much less on dense and basket (about 14 to 11 and 12 to 9 ms).
- The biggest phone phase is offscreen: 3.4 to 3.5 ms a tick on dense and basket, about a quarter to 30 % of the step, compared with "up to 1.3 ms" on desktop. It costs more than the whole contacts+rings+terrain group there. Next come fusion (2.8 ms dense, 4.9 ms moving), train_follow (1.1 to 1.6 ms), frontier (0.5 to 1.5 ms; 20 % of the quiet fixture), train_steer (up to 1.3 ms) and loop_start_queue (0.3 to 0.46 ms).
- Within the solver, contacts is the largest phase (1.0 to 3.1 ms), followed by rings and terrain. doors (0.4 to 0.6 ms) is notable for a solver phase.
- Phone/desktop absolute ratio: not computed (the desktop absolute µs per phase were not at hand, only the shares). Offscreen is about 2.7x desktop's 1.3 ms ceiling.
- stress-dense is at 30 fps for its first minute (tick about 15 ms, about 1.8 ticks a frame), then 59 fps once the crowd has parked. stress-moving stays at about 25 fps throughout.

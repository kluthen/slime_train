# Performance session on the reference phone, 2026-09-30

A hand-played session on the reference phone, measured from the debug
overlay. It is the first time the full game (the test level with its whole
population, chunks 0–21) was measured on a phone.
Next to it: the [independent review](2026-09-30-independent-review.md) of
where the time goes, and chunk 22's measurements in `docs/dev/README.md`
"Chunk 22".

## In short

- **The reference phone misses 60 fps by a wide margin in normal play.**
  - Section 1 runs at 35–47 fps.
  - Section 2 runs at 20–35 fps.
  - Section 3 runs at 15 fps on the shelves, and 4–5 fps while basket 3
    fills and after it is full.
- **Any crowd of slimes on screen collapses the frame rate:**
  - about 105 awake slimes piled at the start give 2–3 fps;
  - about 60 slimes in and around basket 3 give 4–5 fps.
- **The debug labels alone cost a lot:** 36–38 fps with them off, 11–14 fps
  with them on, in the same scenes.
- **Heat played no part.** The phone went from 33.6 to 35.8 °C and reached
  only light throttling (thermal status 1).
- **The chunk 1 spike gave a wrong impression.** It ran 200 slimes at
  48–53 fps on this same phone, but in a different regime: one tick per
  frame, a lighter solver, no game logic. See the review, "Why the spike
  looked fast".

## Method and its limits

- The debug overlay's bar shows:
  - the fps (`Engine.get_frames_per_second()`, an average over about 1 s);
  - woken / available;
  - the slime counts, "on screen : simulated : off screen".
- A script on the desktop took a screenshot every 15 s with
  `adb exec-out screencap` and logged `dumpsys battery` (temperature) and
  `dumpsys thermalservice` (thermal status).
- 41 usable screenshots; the 42nd was cut off when the phone was unplugged
  at 15:54.
- Each reading is one instant, not an average over the 15 s between
  screenshots.
- The overlay's counts don't say how many bodies the solver works on:
  - "on screen" slimes are all simulated;
  - "simulated" counts only the unparked slimes outside the view;
  - "off screen" counts the parked ones.
  - Nothing shows which slimes are active and which are at rest. A pile at
    rest in a basket and an awake pile look the same in these counts.
- There are no frame-time percentiles and no split between tick time and
  drawing time. The perf log chunk 22 adds (`--perf-log`,
  `tools/android/perf.sh`) will measure these next time.

## What happened, in order

1. **Launch with an old save (15:43–15:45).**
   - The phone held a save from an older build of the test level (level
     version 1): 199 sleepers and 1 train slime.
   - Loading migrated it to version 2. About 100 sleepers whose spots no
     longer exist were made "lost" and sent **awake** to the start of the
     loop: 103 train slimes and 94 sleepers afterwards.
   - The resulting pile of about 105 slimes at the start ran at
     **2–3 fps**.
   - The screen-pinning dialog showed at launch, as designed.
2. **Reset (15:45:46), then section 1 at 45–47 fps (15:46–15:47).** The
   debug bar's Reset started the level fresh.
3. **Labels on (15:48–15:49).** Turning the debug labels on took the fps
   from 36 to 11–14 with similar counts. Turning them off brought back
   35–38.
4. **Section 1's basket, section 2 (15:49–15:50).** 25–35 fps; 20 fps at
   section 2's gate with 31 simulated.
5. **Section 3 (15:50–15:54).**
   - 15 fps on the right shelves (79 on screen).
   - Then **4–5 fps** from the moment the camera framed basket 3
     (zoom 0.80): while it filled, when it was full (quota 60), and during
     the celebration.
   - After the basket was full, slimes piled on top of it and rolled over
     it. The full basket keeps its slimes: that is issue 24.3.
   - Leaving the basket area brought 17 fps back (15:53:57).

## What the numbers say

1. **The collapse follows the crowd the solver works on, not the slime
   total.**
   - 200 slimes with 190 parked run at 45–47 fps.
   - About 105 awake slimes in one pile run at 2–3 fps.
   - About 60 slimes in a basket that keeps waking run at 4–5 fps.
2. **The frame rate drops in two steps, 13–20 fps and 2–5 fps.** The review
   explains this with the fixed step's catch-up (up to 8 ticks per frame,
   `src/main.gd:65`). Once a tick costs more than a 60th of a second, each
   frame runs several ticks and the frame rate falls off a cliff.
3. **Basket 3's scene stays at 4–5 fps with only 3–16 "simulated" off
   screen.** The review's hypothesis: every release from the fired basket
   wakes the whole pile before it can rest again, so about 60 bodies stay
   active. Chunk 22 is checking this with an active count.
4. **Drawing matters, but it isn't the main cost.** The debug labels
   (4 strings for each of the 200 slimes, no culling) cost 20–25 fps on
   their own. Chunk 22 is splitting frame time into tick time and drawing
   time.
5. **47 fps at a fresh start with 2 slimes on screen** is below 60 even when
   almost nothing moves. There is a floor of work over all 200 bodies every
   tick (the review's point D), possibly beating against vsync.
6. **Heat is not the cause**: this phone throttled only lightly.

## Issues this session raised

Logged for chunk 24 (playtest issues, round 2), next to 24.1–24.3:

- **24.4 Migration wakes sleepers.** A save from an older level version
  turned about 100 sleepers into awake slimes piled at the loop start. They
  should stay asleep, placed by their stable IDs.
- **24.5 A big awake pile collapses the phone** (2–3 fps). This belongs to
  chunk 22 and chunk 5N, and to 24.4, which caused it.
- **24.6 The debug labels are too expensive** to use on the phone: cull them
  to the view and cache them. Always measure with labels off.
- **24.3 (already open) A full basket keeps its slimes.** The screenshots
  show slimes piling on and rolling over full basket 3. The review links the
  release mechanism to the celebration's 4 fps.

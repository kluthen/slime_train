# Chunk 22e measured: the local wake and the hold, 2026-10-01

Desktop measurements of chunk 22e (D145, D146): the train hop counters,
the local wake, the hold (its crowd check and its jam check), "may rest"
and the celebration waking resting train slimes. Before and after, on the
two bowl fixtures and on two light ones, with the first calibration of
the hold's numbers and a probe of why most holds end at their cap.
Every number comes from the `PERF` lines or from the headless probes'
logs; nothing is read off a screen. All the logs, the probes and the hash
lists are in [`2026-10-01-chunk-22e/`](2026-10-01-chunk-22e/).
Next to it: `docs/dev/README.md`, "Chunk 22d" (the counters and the
largest awake cluster) and "Chunk 22b: drawing" (the drawing parts).

## In short

- **The pile no longer wakes whole.** On basket 3's drain
  (`s3-basket-59of60`) the Physics count fell from 80 to 51 on average
  and the largest awake cluster from 50 to 24. The probe counts 0
  whole-pile wakes after the change, against 6 before (52 to 59 slimes
  each).
- **The bowl's futile hops barely moved.** In `stress-moving` the Physics
  count fell 8 % (134 to 123), hops fell 31 %, but the short-hop share
  stayed at 95 % (96.1 to 95.2 %). In `s3-basket-59of60` no train slime
  holds at all on this seed, and the share stayed at 77 %.
- **The hold mostly waits for its cap.** 86 % of hold ends are at the 5 s
  cap. The crowd check almost never fires: what it counts is the train
  queue itself, about 24 slimes at the back, under its 30. The jam check
  starts 92 % of the holds (331 of 361), and 288 of the 292 holds that
  reach the cap are still jammed. The back of a queue hops freely while
  the jammed middle waits.
- **The tick:** `s3-basket-59of60` 7.27 to 6.68 ms (−8 %); `stress-moving`
  9.68 to 10.17 ms (+5 %): the hold's checks cost about 0.8 ms a tick in
  that crowd, more than the 8 % fewer Physics slimes save. `gate2-open` and
  `fresh` are unchanged (same hashes, same counts).
- **D138's budget, estimated for the phone:** the simulation still misses
  8 ms per tick in both bowl fixtures (`stress-moving` about 21 ms cold,
  `s3-basket-59of60` about 14 ms cold). Drawing is unchanged by 22e: within
  4 ms cold, over it throttled.

## Method

### The trees measured

22e's steps were measured as uncommitted working-tree states on top of
commit df4c4eb, the commit 22e builds on. Four states were measured:

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| **BEFORE** | df4c4eb plus step 1 only: the PERF line's `hops` and `short_hops` counters (`Train.hops_taken`, `short_hops_taken`). Read only: the 17 fixtures' state hashes equal df4c4eb's. |
| **AFTER** | The first full 22e tree: the counters, the local wake, the hold (crowd check and jam check), may rest, the celebration waking resting train slimes, the save key for a hold. |
| **AFTER2** | AFTER plus a faster fusion query (`Fusion.counts_toward_fusion`, a cached set instead of a scan over the contacts). Measured on `stress-moving` only. |
| **AFTER-FINAL** | The final 22e tree: AFTER2 plus the fix that wakes a resting holder when LoopStart moves it. |

The four BEFORE runs of `stress-moving` and `s3-basket-59of60` were made
when the working tree held step 1 only. The `gate2-open` and `fresh` BEFORE
runs were made later, in a separate git worktree of df4c4eb with the same
step 1 applied, so the same code.

### The runs

Each run is a windowed desktop run of the debug build through
`tools/perf_slow.sh`, which plays a test-level fixture in test mode with
**seed 1** (its fixed default) and `--perf-log=2` (a `PERF` line every 2 s
of real time). A run lasts 52 s (40 s plus 12 s for the start) and
logs 25 `PERF` lines from about t = 3 s; every line counts, the start
included, as in the summary. Labels off, no other Godot running. Two
modes:

- **full speed:** `tools/perf_slow.sh --full-speed --max-fps=60 --seconds=40 <fixture>`.
  The desktop's own speed, capped at 60 fps, so a frame holds one tick
  as on a 60 Hz phone. At one tick a frame the simulation repeats exactly
  from run to run: the counts are the behaviour, only the times vary.
- **slowed:** `tools/perf_slow.sh --pin=main --seconds=40 <fixture>`.
  After 3 s of loading, the game's main thread alone is pinned to one CPU
  core shared with two busy loops, so a tick costs about 2.7 times more:
  a stand-in for a frame that can't keep up, not the phone's numbers. The
  frame rate isn't capped, so in light scenes it runs at 200 to 400 fps
  with a tick every 5 to 7 frames. When a tick costs more than a frame,
  the fixed step runs up to 2 ticks a frame and the run covers fewer
  game seconds than real ones (shown as "game s" below).

### The headless probes

Three kinds of throwaway GDScript probe (SceneTree scripts, `godot --headless -s`,
kept in the folder as evidence, not part of the game) load a fixture in
test mode on **seed 1** and step the simulation 2400 ticks (40 s of game
time), headless, so their numbers are exact and repeat:

- **The pile probe** ([`probes/`](2026-10-01-chunk-22e/probes/)):
  `probe_pile_wakes.gd` for BEFORE (with a temporary instrumentation of
  `SlimeBodies`, `instrumentation-slime_bodies.patch`, applied in a
  separate worktree only, to log what woke each slime), and
  `probe_pile_wakes_after.gd` for AFTER (no instrumentation). Per second:
  the Physics, resting and in-basket counts, the slimes going from resting
  to active and their state, the whole-pile wakes (11 or more in one
  tick), the largest awake cluster, catches and releases, basket 3's
  phase, holding and resting train slimes.
- **The sweep probe** ([`sweep/`](2026-10-01-chunk-22e/sweep/)):
  `probe_calib.gd` (`probe_seed.gd`: the same on another seed), run in a
  separate worktree of the AFTER2 tree with only the hold's three numbers
  changed; `--parts` times each part of a tick.
- **The queue probe** ([`queue-probe/`](2026-10-01-chunk-22e/queue-probe/)):
  `queue_probe.gd` replicates `Simulation.step()` (same calls, same order)
  and records, right before `Train.steer`, every train slime whose hop is
  decided that tick: what the crowd check counts and what it is made of,
  whether the jam check fails, its place in the queue, then the outcome.
  Its run ends on the same state hash as a plain run (`qp-plain-stress.log`,
  `qp-stress.log`: 886 hops, the same hash). `qp_analyze.py` makes the
  tables.

### Same behaviour where it should be

Every fixture of the test level (17) ran 600 and 2400 ticks headless in
test mode on **seed 909**, and the final state hashes were compared
([`hashes/`](2026-10-01-chunk-22e/hashes/): `df4c4eb-600.txt`,
`df4c4eb-2400.txt`, `after-*.txt`, `final-*.txt`):

```sh
godot --headless --no-header -- --test-mode --level=test --fixture=<fixture> --seed=909 --run-ticks=600 | grep '^STATE'
```

- **Against df4c4eb, only `stress-moving` and `s3-basket-59of60` changed**,
  at 600 and 2400 ticks. The 15 others are identical: no slime holds in
  them, and their resting piles never take a wake that differs between
  waking the whole pile and waking only the touched slimes.
- **AFTER-FINAL equals AFTER on all 17**, at 600 and 2400 ticks: the
  fusion query fix and the LoopStart fix change no behaviour on these
  fixtures.
- Step 1's counters alone (BEFORE) left all 17 hashes equal to df4c4eb's.

## Why the hold rarely ends before its cap

The user's playtest of 22e (recorded in D147): slimes at the back of the
bowl kept trying to hop with many slimes in front of them, where only the
front should move.
The queue probe answers it on `stress-moving`, (30,240,24), seed 1,
2400 ticks ([`queue-probe/`](2026-10-01-chunk-22e/queue-probe/):
`queue_probe.gd`, `qp-stress.csv`, `qp-stress-analysis.txt`, `qp-s3.csv`,
`qp-s3-analysis.txt`, `qp-*.log`).

A slime's place in the queue is the number of train slimes ahead of it
along the loop within 300 px: the front (0 to 2), 3 to 7, 8 to 14, the
back (15 or more). "Count" is what the crowd check counts.

| Outcome | 0–2 | 3–7 | 8–14 | 15+ | Count, mean / median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hop, no hold | 58 | 90 | 120 | 285 | 16.4 / 19 (back: 24.2 / 24) |
| Hold started | 0 | 0 | 15 | 370 | 22.5 / 22 |
| Re-check failed (still holding) | 0 | 0 | 133 | 2921 | 20.8 / 21 |
| Hold ended, a re-check passed | 0 | 0 | 0 | 41 | 25.1 / 25 |
| Hold ended at the cap | 0 | 0 | 15 | 277 | 20.8 / 21 |

- **The crowd check almost never fires.** What it counts is the train
  queue itself: 100 % train slimes, about 95 % of them the queue ahead
  within 450 px along the loop. The count reaches 35 at most; it is above
  30 at only 30 hold starts and 53 re-checks. At the back of a queue it is
  about 24, under 30.
- **The jam check does the holding.** Of 361 hold starts with a failing
  check, 331 are the jam alone, 28 the crowd alone, 2 both; of the failed
  re-checks, 3001 the jam alone, 7 the crowd, 46 both. 24 more holds
  started in the same tick as the holder ahead of them (the order in which
  `Train.steer` takes the slimes).
- **The cap ends a hold that is still blocked:** 288 of the 292 holds that
  reach the cap are still jammed then. Only 41 holds end because a
  re-check passed, all at the back, after 180 ticks (median).
- **So the back of the queue hops freely** (285 hops with no hold, the
  count about 24, no holder right ahead of its target), **while the middle,
  held by the jam, waits out the 5 s and then hops into it.** The front
  never holds (0 holds in the first 8 places), but neither does it go
  first: almost every place hops.
- **For the crowd check, the cause is its threshold, not where it
  looks.** Counting
  "ahead along the loop" instead of the half-plane on the target's side of
  the slime's centre flips only 19 to 28 hold starts and 7 re-checks: the
  half-plane is not the cause. At HOLD_CROWD 15, 284 back-of-queue hops and
  32 more in places 8 to 14 would become holds, and none at the front
  (whose count is about 1, and about 6 in places 3 to 7). But the sweep
  shows that at 15 the holds still end at the cap (444 of 460), so a
  lower threshold alone moves the delay, not the order: what holds is the
  jam, and what releases is the cap.
- `s3-basket-59of60` on seed 1: no hold at all; 510 hops, count 10.5 on
  average, 30 at most, 100 % train slimes.

**For 22f** (proposed, what these facts support):
- End a hold from the front: a holder goes when the way ahead clears (the
  slime ahead has hopped), not when a timer runs out; the cap as a safety
  net only, staggered so holds begun together don't end together (the 26
  woken in one tick).
- Don't fire a capped hop into a slime that is still blocked (288 of 292).
- Count what is in the hop's own path, resting slimes and holders
  included (today's check leaves resting slimes out): D147's hop
  corridor; or, as the fallback, HOLD_CROWD 15 (the front unaffected, the
  back would hold).
- Log the hold on the `PERF` line (holders, hold ends by cause), so the
  windowed runs and the phone show it.

  `PERF` line can't show it.
- **The outlet is blocked on 99.4 % of release-due ticks,** so basket 3
  releases a slime every 2 to 5 s instead of every 0.3 s (item 24.3).
- **Holds begun together end together:** 26 train slimes woke in one
  tick when their 5 s caps ran out.
- At full speed capped at 60 fps the runs repeat exactly: AFTER and
  AFTER-FINAL give identical counts on both bowl fixtures.

## What couldn't be measured

- **The phone itself.** Every phone number here is an estimate from the
  desktop and chunk 1's factors; the phone's GPU time is unknown on this
  renderer.
- **Basket firing and releases** are not on the `PERF` line; only the
  headless probe shows them.
- **Holds** are not on the `PERF` line either (the probes count them); the
  windowed runs can't show how many train slimes hold or why.
- **The largest awake cluster** is sampled once per 2 s line, so a stretch
  above 20 is known to about 2 s, and what the cluster is made of (a pile,
  a queue) isn't recorded.
- **The slowed runs** run fewer game seconds than real ones when a tick
  outlasts a frame, so their counts are not comparable line by line.

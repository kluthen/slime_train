# The geyser at the return route's end (experiment, 2026-10-06)

EXPERIMENT, branch `exp/geyser` (D157 (5), O117). Not in the spec, no ATD
atom. The code: `src/sim/geyser.gd`; the probe: `tools/geyser_probe.gd`; the
tests: `tests/unit/test_geyser.gd`; how it works: `docs/dev/README.md`,
"The geyser (experiment)".

A train slime that reaches the return route's end (its progress wraps past
the loop's end) is launched high, with a wide spread, so arrivals don't pile
up at the loop's start (level rule 24). Three phases:

1. Variant A (solo: launch from where it arrives, land 100 to 240 px along
   the loop inside a split zone) and B (carry: the jet also launches the
   train slimes in its column).
2. Variant C ("high and wide": lifted above the pile first, landing 150 to
   700 px, a base slime past the split zone too) and D ("wide by rate": the
   spots scored by their 50 px bin's recent landings, 150 to 750 px).
3. The user kept C (2026-10-06). A, B and D are removed; C got landing
   limits (below) and was measured again on main's O91 contacts fix.

All runs: `s3-basket-59of60`, seeds 1 and 2, 14,000 ticks, headless, native
tick unless said (the arrivals start about tick 9000). "hold": the camera
pinned on the start from tick 9000 (`--hold-view=720,361 --hold-from=9000`);
without it the idle camera follows a launched slime away and the start
parks, which empties the measured cluster for the wrong reason.

## Phase 3: C with landing limits (this build)

### The limits

Every drawn spot (10 per arrival, 150 to 700 px along the loop) is turned
down when it is:

- **gate**: at or past the first gate along the loop, or on a slide;
- **zone**: outside every split zone, for a fused slime only. A base slime
  may land past the start's zone. What the zone does with arrivals: it
  splits fused slimes (SplitZones) and stops the slimes inside it from
  fusing (Fusion). Nothing else. So for a base slime, landing past the zone
  only skips the walk out of it. It may fuse there at once, as any train
  slime leaving the zone does. A fused arrival lands inside the zone and
  splits there;
- **ledge**: under or beside a ledge that rule 22 (b) guards. In the code
  such a ledge is a terrain piece a sleeper rests on (LevelData.sleepers +
  the terrain outline under the sleeper, as the level checker's
  `LevelRulesStart.called_ledges` finds them; the simulation keeps no
  ledge record). The test is: some of the piece is over the ring's top
  within the ring's width plus 4 px either side, and the piece's top is
  within a called base slime's reach (`FreeSlimes.max_rise`, about 133 px)
  of the ground. On the test level this covers FirstLedge, about 245 to
  390 px along the loop;
- **route**: off the loop's own route. Coming down the spot's column, the
  slime would rest on the terrain under it, or on the first slime in that
  column (a slime in the air is counted where it comes down). That resting
  centre must lie within `Train.OFF_ROUTE` (36 px) of the route point, as
  the train's own "knocked off the route" test does. A spot with no ground
  under it is turned down the same way. This is what keeps arrivals off
  the queue stacked 80 to 130 px above the loop;
- **flight**: the flight comes within 4 px of the terrain;
- **room** (parked arrivals only, no flight): the ring would overlap
  another slime's.

**Fallback:** if every draw is turned down, the arrival is neither lifted
nor launched. That is the plain arrival: it carries on from the loop's
start as without the geyser. A parked arrival stays in the proxies'
single file.

### Reading

- **The limits work.** No launched arrival comes down on the queue or on
  FirstLedge any more. Of the off-the-loop landings, 0 (s1) and 4 (s2) are
  launched slimes, against 68 to 81 in phase 2. The other 13 and 15 are
  plain arrivals sitting in the pocket behind the start (x 58 to 230), as in
  the off runs (21 to 22).
- **With them, C does little on this fixture.** When the arrivals come, the
  stretch from 150 to 700 px is taken. Probed at ticks 9600 and 10800, every
  spot 25 px apart had a slime in its column: the resting centre would be
  46 to 120 px above the route. Only 1 spot of 23 was free each time. So 56 to
  64 % of the arrivals find no spot and arrive plainly. The ones launched
  clear the start in 78 ticks (median) instead of about 1000. But the
  cluster stays the same (clu_max 119 to 121 against 117 to 118, mean -5 %),
  and so do the departures past 240 px (95 to 96 against 95 to 97). The
  start is somewhat emptier: near_mean -11 %, pocket -15 %, back -20 %.
- Phase 2's gains came from putting slimes on top of the queue. Once that is
  forbidden, there is no room left to spread arrivals into. The limit is the
  train's take-up downstream: about 3 slimes per 600 ticks past 750 px,
  against about 17 arrivals. As phase 2 concluded, a geyser only helps on
  top of pacing. The return route's end would have to meter its arrivals to
  that take-up (D150-like), with the geyser spreading the metered ones.
- **Cost.** The tick went up by about 0.3 ms (3.45 to 3.47 ms off, 3.72 to
  3.80 ms on). That is near the run-to-run noise on this shared desktop:
  phase 2's C came out 0.2 ms faster than off. Each arrival checks 10 spots,
  and each spot goes over every terrain segment and every slime.

### Tests and fixture hashes

- `tests/unit/test_geyser.gd`: 19 tests, 90 asserts, pass on both ticks.
  They cover: the launch, coming down on its spot, the seeded draws, its
  own stream, the off switch, a base slime landing past the zone, a fused
  one only inside a zone, an overhang, the emptiest kept spot, never onto
  a slime or a spot with no ground, never under a guarded ledge (and one out
  of reach is fine), never at or past a gate, the plain arrival when no
  spot is left, parked arrivals, the lift.
- Full suite (`tools/test.sh -gdisable_colors`): native pass 1523/1524 and
  GDScript pass 126/126, exit code 1. The one failure was
  `test_offscreen_single_file.gd`,
  `test_a_parked_slime_on_the_slide_waits_behind_the_parked_slime_ahead`.
  It checks the single file across the loop's end. With the geyser, the
  parked slime behind wraps and is placed, on purpose, on a free spot 150 to
  700 px along, ahead of the one in front. The test now turns the geyser off
  and says why; the placement itself is covered in `test_geyser.gd`. After
  the fix the full suite passes: native 1524/1524, GDScript 126/126, exit
  code 0.
- Fixture hashes (seed 909, 600 and 2400 ticks, the 18 fixtures, the
  geyser on): all 36 equal the table in `docs/dev/native.md`. No fixture
  reaches the return route's end within 2400 ticks.

### Risks and what a kept version still has to settle (O117)

- On a crowded start, most arrivals are refused, and C is then mostly the
  plain arrival. It needs pacing to matter.
- A guarded ledge is found from the sleepers' positions at load. A ledge
  whose sleeper is gone still counts, because the layout doesn't change. A
  sleeper resting on the main ground would make its overhangs count as
  ledges.
- The `route` test drops each spot straight down its column, but the real
  flight comes in at an angle. A spot that was free at launch can fill
  during the flight of about 1 s: s2 had 4 such launched landings off the
  loop.
- The trigger is "laps went up", so any wrap counts.
- The idle camera follows launched slimes away from the start, and the
  start then parks. This is a UX effect to decide on.
- Base slimes landing past the split zone fuse at once there. That is a
  design choice to confirm.
